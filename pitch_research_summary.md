## Overview

*Summa of the Gap* is a unified interdisciplinary treatise organized around a single principle: **the gap does not close** (δ = 0 is dead; the gap is life). It builds a formal architecture across six volumes and multiple appendices, with every claim explicitly labeled by epistemic status.

---

## Volume I: The Epistemological Engine

Establishes the foundational method. All knowledge claims are tagged:
- **[P]** Proven (formal proof or theorem)
- **[E]** Empirical (data-supported, reproducible)
- **[C]** Conjectured (plausible, not yet demonstrated)
- **[I]** Interpretation (framing or philosophical stance)

This is both a writing discipline and a ready-made evaluation framework — models or systems can be audited against the same taxonomy.

---

## Volume II: The Physics of the Boundary

Extends the Connes noncommutative geometry program. Derives **conditional spectral gap closures** — conditions under which the gap in a Dirac-type operator spectrum vanishes. The core result: the gap closes only under strict operator constraints that correspond to physical boundary conditions. Otherwise, a residual spectral gap persists.

Implications: fundamental limits on spectral resolution in quantum systems, and a geometric interpretation of the P vs NP boundary.

---

## Volume III: The Geometry of Computation

Reformulates computational complexity spectrally. Decision problems map onto eigenvalue spectra of self-adjoint operators on Hilbert space. The P/NP distinction becomes a **spectral gap condition**: if the verification operator has bounded spectral radius, the classes collapse; otherwise they separate.

Connects to: quantum geometric tensor analysis, phase transitions in optimization landscapes, and scaling-law behavior in large models.

---

## Volume IV: The Architecture of the Subject

Formalizes consciousness as **incomplete self-modeling**. Phenomenal states are the irreducible gap between a system's total state space and its internal representational capacity. Key result: under mild constraints, a system cannot fully model its own modeling — there is a persistent representational deficit.

Relevance to AI: this provides a formal bound on interpretability. A sufficiently self-referential model will always contain structure opaque to its own introspection.

---

## Volume V: The Applied Monad

Applies the framework to concrete systems:
- **Spectral psychiatry:** Mental states as operator spectra rather than DSM categories. Continuous, dynamic, measurable.
- **Radical-pair / cardiac physiology:** Magnetic-field-effect predictions in heart tissue — an experimental protocol connecting quantum biology to macroscopic function.
- **Cybernetics:** Feedback architectures for systems that must operate across irreducible gaps.

---

## Volume VI: The Ontological Horizon

Synthesizes the program. The gap is not a failure of theory — it is the condition of possibility for structure, life, and intelligence. Closes with open questions, build instructions, and φ-Map tables (cross-reference of results to epistemic status).
