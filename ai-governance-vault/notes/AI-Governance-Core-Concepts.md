# AI Governance — Core Concepts

## Governance vs. Ethics vs. Regulation

| Layer | Focus | Key Question |
|---|---|---|
| **Ethics** | Principles, values | What should AI NOT do? |
| **Regulation** | Rules, enforcement | What must AI comply with? |
| **Governance** | Institutions, processes, power | Who decides, and how? |

Ulnicane's argument: the debate has over-indexed on ethics principles while under-scrutinizing governance, politics, and policy.

## Key Terms

### Polycentric Governance
Multiple overlapping centers of decision-making in collaborative relationships. AI governance is inherently polycentric: states, IOs, companies, civil society all govern simultaneously.

### Governance Fix
Narrow, technocratic approach to governing emerging technology (Ulnicane 2025). Focuses on risk management while neglecting purpose/direction of innovation. Limits public participation.

### Ethics Washing
Powerful actors emphasize ethics guidelines to avoid or delay binding regulation. Creates appearance of responsibility without accountability.

### Responsible Innovation (RRI)
Democratic and participatory co-shaping of technology for social benefit. Anticipates impacts, includes diverse publics, reflects on purposes.

### AI Space Race Rhetoric
Frames AI as zero-sum competition among superpowers. Prioritizes narrow economic interests over broader societal needs, pressures governments to regulate less.

## Core Tension

```
Ethics Guidelines (popular, non-binding)
        vs.
Regulation (cautious, binding)
        =
Governance Gap (who governs, with what interests?)
```
