# Governance, Politics & Policies of Artificial Intelligence

**Inga Ulnicane (2021)**

## The Core Question

> What are the roles of government in shaping AI development and use? What are the roles of society? How and why are AI strategies developed? Which ideas, interests, and values do they prioritize — and which do they leave out?

## Research Design

- **49 AI policy documents** analyzed from: national governments, international organizations, consultancies, think tanks, civil society
- **Interdisciplinary conceptual framework**: AI as emerging technology — fast growth, considerable impacts, uncertainty, hype, positive & negative expectations
- **Over 3 years** of research within the Human Brain Project (HBP)

## Key Findings

### 1. Framing Governance
- AI policy documents frame governance as way to resolve public controversies
- Countries learn from each other and from OECD, World Economic Forum
- Benefits framed not only as economic growth but as Grand Societal Challenges / UN SDGs

### 2. Roles of Government
- **Promoting AI** — investment, infrastructure, skills
- **Mitigating risks** — safety, security, bias
- **Encouraging stakeholder involvement** — multi-stakeholder forums
- Moves beyond "big vs. small government" debate toward nuanced understanding

### 3. Multi-Stakeholder Challenge
- AI policy documents assign important role to multi-stakeholder forums
- In practice: hard to ensure balanced representation
- Risk of **capture by well-resourced, organized vested interests**

### 4. Beyond Ethics Guidelines → Good Governance
- Enthusiasm for ethics guidelines vs. cautious attitude toward regulation
- **"Ethics washing"**: powerful actors use ethics guidelines to avoid/delay binding regulation
- Alternatives: state/non-state cooperation, international research collaboration, **Responsible Innovation**

## Key Concepts Introduced

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Framing governance** | How policy documents construct the problem and solution space for AI |
| **Ethics washing** | Ethics guidelines as substitute for binding regulation |
| **Multi-stakeholder capture** | Well-resourced interests dominating participatory forums |
| **Good governance** | Beyond ethics + regulation: cooperation, coordination, RRI |
| **Responsible Innovation** | Democratic co-shaping of technology for social benefit |
