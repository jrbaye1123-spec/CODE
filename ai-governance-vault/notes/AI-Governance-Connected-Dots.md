# AI Governance — Connected Dots

> "The question is not just 'how to regulate AI?' but 'who governs, with what interests, and for whose benefit?'"

## The Central Claim

Ulnicane's research programme (2020-2025) argues that AI governance debates have over-indexed on **ethics principles** while under-scrutinizing **governance, politics, and policies**. The 35 papers in this vault validate this claim: each reveals a facet of how power, interests, and framing shape AI governance.

## The Deeper Pattern

Every paper in this vault addresses a version of the same question:
> Who governs AI? Through what institutions? Serving whose interests? With what framings?

Ulnicane's answer: we are building a **polycentric but narrow** governance architecture — dominated by developed countries, large tech companies, and technocratic risk-management — that systematically excludes the public and neglects questions of purpose and direction.
