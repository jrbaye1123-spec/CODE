# OECD as Norm-Setter in AI Governance

The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) has emerged as the central node in the nascent global AI governance regime.

## Why the OECD?

- **Epistemic authority**: Produces authoritative analysis and measurement
- **Norm-setting power**: OECD AI Principles (2019) adopted by 40+ countries
- **Convening power**: Brings together governments, industry, civil society
- **Existing infrastructure**: AI Policy Observatory, Going Digital project
- **Network effects**: Countries learn from each other through OECD platforms

## Schmitt's Analysis (2021)

[[Mapping global AI governance]] uses a 2×2 matrix to map actors:
- Existing vs. new governance architecture
- State-driven vs. multi-stakeholder

The OECD occupies a central position: leveraging existing architecture with multi-stakeholder legitimacy.

## Limitations

- Membership limited to developed economies
- Non-binding (soft law)
- Can be seen as reinforcing Western-centric governance
