# Mapping Global AI Governance

**Schmitt (2021)**

## Core Contribution

Describes and evaluates the emerging global AI governance architecture. Organizes actors and initiatives in a **2×2 matrix**:
- Row: Existing vs. New governance architecture
- Column: State-driven vs. Multi-stakeholder

## Key Findings

1. **OECD as central node**: Holds considerable epistemic authority and norm-setting power
2. **First signs of consolidation**: Nascent regime emerging from fragmented landscape
3. **Existing frameworks preferred**: Tendency to address AI within existing governance architecture rather than create new institutions
4. **IO agency**: International organizations show high levels of agency in addressing AI policy

## Bridge to Ulnicane

- Ulnicane's [[Governance, politics and policies of Artificial Intelligence]] analyzes the *content* of 49 AI policy documents; Schmitt maps the *architecture* of who produces them
- Schmitt's "nascent regime" → Ulnicane's "governance fix": the regime is consolidating but in a narrow, technocratic direction
