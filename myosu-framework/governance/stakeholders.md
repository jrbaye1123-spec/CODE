# Stakeholder Impact Map

## Direct Beneficiaries

- **John** — Extended cognitive capacity with preserved authorship.
- **Collaborators** — Reliable provenance on shared research artifacts.
- **Reviewers/Editors** — Verifiable attribution trail for published claims.

## Potentially Burdened or Excluded

- **Non-English researchers** — Retrieval bias may systematically exclude
  non-English sources. Mitigated by quarterly retrieval fairness audits.
- **Non-traditional knowledge traditions** — Oral histories, community
  archives, indigenous knowledge may be underranked by prestige-biased
  retrieval. Mitigated by retrieval dissent agent (design consideration).
- **Cited authors** — Agent extraction may misrepresent or decontextualize
  their work. Mitigated by provenance visibility and verbatim extraction
  requirements.
- **Research subjects** — Personal data in sources must be minimized.
  Mitigated by data minimization policy and retention schedule.

## Expected Benefits

- Preservation of intellectual provenance over time
- Detection of contradiction and tension before publication
- Systematic awareness of retrieval exclusion patterns
- Defensible authorship under scrutiny

## Plausible Harms

- Silent authorship erosion through ratification-at-scale
- Systematic exclusion of non-Western, non-English sources
- Cross-project context collapse (claim reused without original qualifiers)
- Governance theater (review load exceeds meaningful capacity)
