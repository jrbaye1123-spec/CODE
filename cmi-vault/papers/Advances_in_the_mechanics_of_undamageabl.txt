Abstract
The theory of undamageable materials is extended to general states of deformation and damage.
Undamageable materials are hypothetical materials that do not undergo any damage processes under
the action of forces within the framework of continuum damage mechanics. In these types of materials, the
damage variable remains zero throughout the deformation process. The authors developed previously the
theory of undamageable materials for the one-dimensional case using scalars. In this work, the general
three-dimensional formulation is presented using tensors. In this regard, a system of nonlinear partial
differential equations governing the mechanics of undamageable materials is obtained. An attempt is made
to solve the governing system using one single simplifying assumption. It is hope that the theoretical
formulation presented in this work will open the way for future development of these types of new
materials in practical applications.
Keywords
Damage mechanics, undamageable materials, three dimensional formulation, tensor formulation, higherorder strain energy

Introduction
The subject of continuum damage mechanics was pioneered by Kachanov (1958) and more fully
developed by Lee et al. (1985), Voyiadjis and Kattan (1992, 2005, 2006, 2009), Sidoroﬀ (1981),
Krajcinovic (1996), and Kattan and Voyiadjis (1993, 2001a, 2001b).
In 1958, Kachanov pioneered the concept of eﬀective stress and introduced the topic of continuum damage mechanics. This development was followed by Rabotnov (1969) and by others later
(Kattan and Voyiadjis, 2001a, 2001b; Ladeveze and Lemaitre, 1984; Voyiadjis and Kattan, 2005,
2006, 2009, 2012a). In the framework of continuum damage mechanics, a scalar damage variable ’ is

Research on damage mechanics accelerated rapidly in the past few years (Celentano et al., 2004;

Lee et al., 1985; Lubineau, 2010; Lubineau and Ladeveze, 2008; Luccioni and Oller, 2003; Rice,

damage characterization is to use an entropy generation rate as a damage metric rather than the
damage potential surface (Basaran and Nie, 2004; Basaran and Yan, 1998).
Undamageable materials are hypothetical types of material that were ﬁrst postulated by the
authors in 2012 (Voyiadjis and Kattan, 2012c, 2013, 2014a, 2014b). In these types of materials,
the value of the damage variable remains identically zero throughout the deformation process. Thus,
theoretically these materials cannot be damaged under the action of forces within the context of the
theory of continuum damage mechanics. The full theoretical formulation was developed by the
authors for the one-dimensional case using scalars (Voyiadjis and Kattan, 2012c, 2013, 2014a,
2014b). In this work, the general three-dimensional formulation is developed using tensors.
However, only the governing diﬀerential equations are presented without solution. They are so
complicated that they may be unsolvable at the present time.

The relation of the presented concept of undamageable materials to the ideal locking material

Conclusion
Undamageable materials are four-dimensional materials. There is a transformation from three
dimensions to four dimensions as n goes to inﬁnity. Check Appendix 1 for vector exponentiation
to see how this transformation is proved for vectors – it shows how a vector becomes a matrix as n
goes to inﬁnity. A transformation occurs from a one-dimensional vector into a two-dimensional
matrix.
Undamageable . . .. Value of damage variable remains identically zero throughout the process of
deformation and damage . . .
Extremely large strains – expected and logical . . .
The following are properties of four-dimensional materials that can be deduced based on the
current investigation of the theory of undamageable materials:
(1) The material as n ! 1 must be undamageable.
(2) The value of the stress will remain equal to zero throughout the deformation process, for the
speciﬁc case n ! 1.
(3) The value of the damage variable will be equal to zero also throughout the deformation process.
(4) The undamageable material has zero strain energy. This property is directly derived from property # 2 above.
(5) The undamageable material has nonzero strain values. Thus, the undamageable material is a
type of deformable material, not a rigid body.
(6) The undamageable material is based on the proposed higher-order strain energy function
U ¼ 12  "n taken in the limit when n ! 1.
In this work, the general three-dimensional governing tensorial equations for undamageable
materials have been derived. However, these general equations have not been solved. They are so
complicated such that they may be unsolvable at the present time. However, the scalar case is
solvable and references to the solution of the scalar case are given.
