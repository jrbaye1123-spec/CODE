Abstract. The mathematical theory of inhomogeneities in simple elastic materials is reformulated
in terms of G-structures, thereby allowing for the explicit solution of the homogeneity condition
for a number of special material classes.

Introduction

The mathematical theory of uniform elastic bodies with inhomogeneities was
originally proposed by Noll [1] and further developed by Wang [2]. Using the
language of connections on manifolds they showed that for a uniform elastic
body a connection on the material tangent bundle can be defined consistent
with the given response functional of the body. This material connection,
which is not unique in most cases, became the tool for the analysis and
classification of uniform simple (elastic) bodies. Specifically, the existence of a
flat (integrable) material connection was shown to be equivalent to the
homogeneity of the (uniform) body. It should be noted, however, that the
process of analyzing the material structure of an elastic body is two-fold.
Initially, one needs to ascertain whether or not the given constitutive (response) functional defines a uniform material structure. Only after the material uniformity has been established, and thus material connections become
available, is it possible to address mathematically the question of homogeneity. More specifically, even if the first step is successfully tackled - and the
general solution to such problem is far from being available (see attempt in
[3]) - , in order to answer the second question one would need to determine
whether within the variety of the material connections there exists an integrable one. This may prove to be technically a very difficult task [4].
In this paper we show how one can avoid, at least in some cases, such
difficulties in solving the problem of homogeneity. Assuming that the uniformity of the material body has already been established, we attack the local
homogeneity question by first showing that a definite material G-structure can

3. Material structures

In this section we shall analyze a number of well-known uniform material
structures often used in Elasticity. We shall only be concerned with the local
homogeneity problem, i.e., with the conditions under which a given uniform
elastic body is locally homogeneous.
The structures which we analyze here can be divided into two different
categories. The first category, which is represented by an elastic fluid, an

We start our investigation with an analysis of the so-called Elastic Fluid.
DEFINITION 5. An Elastic Fluid is a uniform elastic body the typical isotropy
group of which is the special linear group SL(3).
It should be noted here that in the case of an elastic fluid it makes sense to
talk about the typical isotropy group as any conjugate to SL(3) is the group
itself.*
It follows from Proposition 2 that there exists an SL(3)-structure (material
structure) naturally associated with any elastic fluid uniform body. The
characteristic object of such a structure is a nowhere zero exterior differential
form (a volume form) on the body manifold B. As it is well known, [8], any
two volume forms (maximal exterior forms) on a closed connected manifold
which yield the same total volume can be transformed into each other by the
pull-back of a diffeomorphism of the manifold. This naturally implies that
every SL(3)-structure on a 3-dimensional orientable manifold is locally flat,
which leads to the following statement.
PROPOSITION 3. Every uniform elastic fluid body is locally homogeneous.
We turn our attention now to the isotropic solid.
