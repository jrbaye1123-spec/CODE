A NON–CONVENTIONAL GAUGE APPROACH
TOWARDS CONTINUUM THEORIES
OF DISSIPATIVE DEFORMATION PROCESSES
M. SCHOLLE
FB 6, Theoretische Physik, Universität Paderborn, Warburger Straße 100
D–33095 Paderborn, Germany
In this paper is presented a powerful method in order to establish Lagrangian
descriptions for various systems in continuums mechanics: The inverse problem of
the second kind is different from traditional approaches (classical inverse problem,
gauge field theories) in many ways. It is based on an inverse treatment of Noether’s
theorem on the one hand and of the area–type balance theorem — a mathematical
procedure similar to Noether’s theorem — on the other hand. In order to make this
procedure unique, an additional methodical requirement for the analytical form of
the Lagrangian is needed which I call the dual property. These considerations give
rise to a general ansatz for the Lagrangian which is applied to elastically deforming
bodies with eigenstresses and viscous fluids.
Keywords. Lagrange formalism, Lie and non–Lie symmetries, volume–type and
area–type balances, dissipation

2

The Inverse Problem of the Second Kind

In continuum physics for various systems a Lagrangian description has not been
established. Traditionally, there are two methodically different ways towards
the construction of a Lagrangian: The conventional inverse problem starts
from a yet established phenomenological description of the system by means
of partial differential equations whereas gauge field theories start from specific
symmetry requirements for the Lagrangian.
In this paper a non–conventional method, the inverse problem of the second
kind, is proposed which is neither the pure traditional inverse problem nor
a pure gauge field theory nor a mixture of both: It starts from the local
balances for energy, linear momentum, angular momentum, center of mass
motion, mass, vortices and topological defects and from the dual property
(see ch. 1.2) of the unknown Lagrangian as an essential methodical postulate.
Looking in an inverse manner at Noether’s theorem on the one hand and at
the area–type balance theorem (see ch. 1.3) on the other hand restrictions on
the analytical structure and on the gauge symmetry group of the unknown
Lagrangian can be found. This allows for the construction of a rough scheme
for the structure of the Lagrangian which can be adapted to specific problems.
