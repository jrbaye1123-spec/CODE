# The Metaphysics of Constitutive Mechanistic Phenomena

**Kaiser & Krickel (2016)**

## Core Contribution

Specifies the ontological nature of constitutive mechanistic phenomena as **"object-involving occurrents."** Distinguishes constitutive mechanistic explanations from etiological ones.

## Key Ideas

- **Constitutive mechanisms** explain phenomena by their components and organization
- **Etiological mechanisms** explain by prior causes
- **Object-involving occurrents**: Phenomena are not just behaviors, but involve the *objects* that exhibit them
- Three criteria: descriptive adequacy, constitutive-etiological distinction, constitution

## Bridge to CMI

This is the philosophical backbone of CMI's "Physical Monad" concept:
- An **object-involving occurrent**: the scaffold IS the degradation process, not just its substrate
- Constitutive vs. etiological: CMI asks "what IS the scaffold?" (constitutive) not just "what caused it to degrade?" (etiological)
- The monad is the *constitutive mechanistic phenomenon* par excellence: its internal organization determines its behavior
