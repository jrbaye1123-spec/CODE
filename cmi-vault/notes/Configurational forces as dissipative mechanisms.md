# Configurational Forces as Dissipative Mechanisms

**Gupta & Markenscoff (2008)**

## Core Contribution

A variational argument proving a **necessary and sufficient condition** for interpreting the work done by configurational forces as net dissipation: the Euler-Lagrange equation associated with the variational integral must hold.

## Key Results

1. Configurational work = net dissipation **iff** Euler-Lagrange equations satisfied
2. Configurational forces relate to: Peach-Koehler force on dislocations, driving traction on phase boundaries, energy release rates in fracture
3. Follows Noether's theorem procedure for elastic bodies
4. Extends to configurational forces in stress space

## Bridge to CMI

- CMI's **Noether charge Q = Tr(F∧F)** is the *conserved* side; Gupta & Markenscoff's configurational work is the *dissipated* side — two faces of Noether's theorem in materials
- The Euler-Lagrange condition is precisely what CMI's Rosen closure (ηγ₀=1) enforces: the system must satisfy its own equations of motion to be self-consistent
- CMI's degradation front as a dissipative process driven by configurational forces mirrors the energy-release-rate picture
