# Evolution in Materio

**Harding, Miller & Rietman**

## Core Idea

Use evolutionary algorithms to **program materials** for non-classical computation. The material's own physics performs the computation — no need to simulate it digitally.

## Key Insights

1. **Physical computation**: Crystal growth, corrosion dendrites, ink dispersion are all computations
2. **Nearest-neighbor only**: No global clock, no central processor — fully distributed
3. **Better than simulation**: Real material dynamics exceed what supercomputers can model
4. **Evolutionary programming**: Use genetic algorithms to configure material parameters for desired computation

## Bridge to CMI

This paper and CMI share a profound philosophical alignment:
- **Materials are not passive substrates** — they have intrinsic dynamics
- **The physics IS the computation** — in CMI, the scaffold's geometry IS the degradation "algorithm"
- **Exploit rather than simulate** — CMI says the scaffold is "the question posed to nature"
- **Self-organization**: Both frameworks see the material as an autonomous system, not a blank slate

The difference: Harding & Miller use *external* evolutionary algorithms to program the material; CMI sees the material as **inherently self-programming** through ∇self.
